1. The App

BuzzBee is a smart alarm clock app that wakes you within a chosen time window using motion-based sleep detection ("Smart Wake"), or at a fixed time, and requires you to complete a short dismiss "mission" (math, tap, shake, clap, or a sustained sound) to turn the alarm off.

2. Important: Alarm Reliability Disclaimer

Please read this section carefully — it matters for an alarm app.

BuzzBee is built to be as reliable as the underlying mobile operating system allows, but it has real, honest limitations you should understand before relying on it for anything important:

  • Background and killed-app behavior. Modern mobile operating systems restrict what apps can do when they are not open. BuzzBee schedules a backup system notification as a safety net, but this notification may use a different (quieter, non-looping) sound than your chosen alarm tone, and if the app has been fully closed (force-quit) by you or the system, BuzzBee cannot guarantee it will launch or ring the same way it does when open.
  • Do Not Disturb, Silent Mode, and system settings. Depending on your device's settings, certain modes may reduce or silence alerts. We recommend testing BuzzBee's behavior with your specific device settings before depending on it for anything time-critical.
  • Smart Wake is a heuristic, not a medical device. Smart Wake estimates sleep depth from motion patterns. It is a best-effort convenience feature, not a clinically validated sleep-tracking or medical tool, and it may not always detect movement accurately.
  • No guarantee of waking you up. While BuzzBee is designed with a hard-deadline fallback specifically so it never rings later than the time you set, we cannot guarantee, and do not warrant, that the App will wake you under all circumstances (for example, extremely low device volume, a malfunctioning device, an operating system bug, or a fully depleted battery).

You are responsible for testing BuzzBee's reliability on your own device before relying on it for anything where oversleeping would have serious consequences (e.g., a flight, an exam, a medical appointment). We recommend keeping a backup alarm method for anything truly critical.
